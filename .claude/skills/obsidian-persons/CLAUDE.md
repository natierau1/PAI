## System Overview

### Philosophy

This system treats **relationships as the foundation of all work**. Projects succeed or fail based on the people involved. By tracking relationship closeness, collaboration patterns, and interaction health, we can:

- Identify strategic partners worth investing in
- Catch relationship issues before they become project blockers
- Discover emerging collaborators who deserve more attention
- Prevent single-point dependencies
- Maintain strong connections with core relationships

### Core Capabilities

1. **People List Management** - Maintain accurate, categorized lists of who you work with
2. **Relationship Tier Assessment** - Objectively evaluate closeness/importance (1-5 scale)
3. **Collaboration Analysis** - Map people to projects, find patterns, identify risks
4. **Person Note Creation** - Individual pages for key relationships with context
5. **Health Monitoring** - Track interaction frequency, flag stale relationships
6. **Strategic Planning** - Identify who to invest in, who to maintain, who to archive

## Relationship Tier Framework

### 5-Tier System

People are categorized by their **closeness** and **importance** to your life and work. Tiers should be based on objective data, not just feelings.

---

### Tier 1: Core (Pivotal to Life & Work)

**Definition:** These people are foundational. Your work and life would be significantly different without them.

**Characteristics:**
- Daily or weekly interaction
- Critical to multiple major projects
- Deep trust and long-term relationship (typically 1+ years)
- Significant impact on business/personal trajectory
- Losing this relationship would require major adjustment
- High emotional and professional investment

**Data signals:**
- Appears in 3+ active projects
- Contact every 1-14 days
- Projects with them have high success rate
- Recurring collaboration over 12+ months
- Decision-making influence

**Maintenance:**
- Proactive contact every 1-2 weeks minimum
- Regular check-ins beyond project needs
- Invest in relationship deepening
- Priority response time
- Share strategic thinking

**Current examples:**
- **Family:** Yvette (wife), Taia, Judah, Caleb, Gabriel (children)
- **Strategic partners:** Kirsty Chadwick (TTRO - multiple opportunities), Ron Dahlgren (Connected IQ - technical foundation), Michael Cosgrave (Glenart - strategic business)

**Typical roles:**
- Life partner, immediate family
- Co-founders, business partners
- Long-term strategic collaborators
- Key advisors with proven track record

- Strong professional relationship
- Would impact success if relationship ended

**Data signals:**

- Reliable delivery and communication
- Projects with them generally succeed
- Mutual value exchange

- Good response priority

### Tier 5: Passive (Past or Minimal Contact)

**Definition:** Historical relationships, past collaborators, or inactive connections.

**Characteristics:**
- No current active projects
- Minimal or no interaction (3+ months)
- Completed/ended project history
- Relationship may reactivate in future
- Low current relevance
- Archived but not forgotten

**Data signals:**
- No appearance in active projects
- Last contact > 90 days
- Project completion or opportunity passed
- No active mutual value exchange
- Relationship on hold

**Maintenance:**
- Passive monitoring only
- Annual check-in (optional)
- Responsive if they reach out
- Consider archiving person note
- Re-tier if relationship reactivates

**Current examples:**

- Declined opportunities
- Moved on to other roles
- Archived business contacts

**Typical roles:**
- Former colleagues
- Past clients
- Completed project teams
- Stale networking contacts

### Tier Movement Signals

**Promotion signals (move up a tier):**
- Appearing in additional projects
- Increasing interaction frequency
- Growing strategic importance
- Proven reliability and value
- Relationship deepening naturally
- Mutual investment increasing

**Demotion signals (move down a tier):**
- Decreasing interaction frequency
- Project completion/ending
- Reduced responsiveness
- Strategic importance declining
- Natural relationship drift
- One-sided effort

**Assessment cadence:**
- Tier 1-2: Review quarterly
- Tier 3: Review every 6 months
- Tier 4-5: Review annually or when reactivated

### Workflow 1: Update People Lists from Active Projects

**Trigger:** After project updates, new projects start, monthly review

**Process:**

```
1. SCAN PROJECT FILES
   - Read all files in /3 Efforts/ON/*.md
   - Read all files in /3 Efforts/Ongoing/*.md
   - Focus on PT* and OPT* files (project/opportunity files)

2. EXTRACT PEOPLE DATA
   For each project file, extract:
   - Team sections (## Team, ## Key Contacts, etc.)
   - People mentioned in References sections
   - Names in meeting notes
   - Contact lists
   - @mentions or [[Person Name]] links

   Capture for each person:
   - Full name
   - Role in project
   - Organization (if mentioned)
   - Project they're involved with

3. CATEGORIZE BY TIER
   For each person found, assess tier using:
   - Number of projects they appear in
   - Roles they play (lead, strategic, support)
   - Known relationship history
   - Interaction frequency (if available from Diary)

   Apply tier framework:
   - 3+ projects + key roles = Tier 1-2 candidate
   - 1-2 projects + active role = Tier 2-3
   - Single project = Tier 3-4
   - Mentioned but no active role = Tier 4-5

4. UPDATE MASTER LIST
   - Open: Atlas Dots/Lists/People - Active Projects.md
   - Organize by:
     * Family (always at top)
     * Project categories (Carbon Plant, EVOLVE, TTRO, etc.)

     * Name with [[wiki link]]
     * Role/title
     * Projects involved
     * Organization
   - Use consistent formatting

5. FLAG CHANGES
   Compare to previous version:
   - New people added
   - People appearing in new projects
   - Tier changes (if tracking in list)
   - People no longer in active projects

6. REPORT SUMMARY
   Provide user with:
   - Total people in active projects
   - New additions
   - Tier promotions/demotions
   - Removed/archived people
   - Recommended person note creation
```

### Workflow 2: Assess Relationship Tier for Specific Person

**Trigger:** "How close am I to [person]?", "Assess my relationship with [person]"

**Process:**

```
1. GATHER DATA
   Search across vault:
   - Project files: grep for [[Person Name]] or "Person Name"
   - Count project appearances
   - Extract roles they play
   - Check Diary/ for interaction dates
   - Review any existing person note

2. ANALYZE PROJECT INVOLVEMENT
   For each project appearance:
   - What role? (Lead, support, client, partner, advisor)
   - Project health/status? (Active, successful, struggling)
   - How critical are they? (Could project continue without them?)
   - How long have they been involved?

3. ANALYZE INTERACTION FREQUENCY
   From Diary entries:
   - Last contact date
   - Typical frequency (weekly, monthly, quarterly)
   - Trend (increasing, stable, decreasing)
   - Initiation pattern (who reaches out first)

4. EVALUATE STRATEGIC IMPORTANCE
   Consider:
   - Impact on business outcomes
   - Access to opportunities/networks
   - Technical/domain expertise uniqueness
   - Trust level and relationship depth
   - Long-term potential

5. ASSIGN TIER
   Apply framework:
   - Tier 1: 3+ projects + weekly contact + critical impact
   - Tier 2: 1-2 projects + bi-weekly contact + high importance
   - Tier 3: 1 project + active phase contact + specific role
   - Tier 4: New/exploratory + occasional contact
   - Tier 5: No active projects + 90+ days since contact

6. PROVIDE RECOMMENDATION
   - Current tier classification
   - Justification with data
   - Relationship health assessment
   - Suggested actions (maintain, deepen, monitor)
   - Tier movement prediction (likely to change?)
```

### Workflow 3: Map People to Projects (Collaboration Analysis)

**Trigger:** "Who am I working with most?", "Show me collaboration patterns"

**Process:**

```
1. CREATE PROJECT-PERSON MATRIX
   Build data structure:
   {
     "Project A": ["Person 1", "Person 2", "Person 3"],
     "Project B": ["Person 1", "Person 4"],
     "Project C": ["Person 2", "Person 5"]
   }

2. CREATE PERSON-PROJECT MATRIX
   Invert the structure:
   {
     "Person 1": ["Project A", "Project B"],
     "Person 2": ["Project A", "Project C"],
     "Person 3": ["Project A"]
   }

3. IDENTIFY MULTI-PROJECT COLLABORATORS
   - Sort people by project count (descending)
   - Flag anyone in 2+ projects
   - These are strategic relationship candidates

4. IDENTIFY SINGLE-POINT DEPENDENCIES
   For critical projects:
   - Find people who are sole contact
   - Check if no backup exists
   - Flag as risk

5. FIND COLLABORATION CLUSTERS
   - People who appear together across projects
   - Indicates strong working relationships
   - Potential for team replication

6. ANALYZE BY TIER
   - How many Tier 1 people per project?
   - Do critical projects have Tier 2+ contacts?
   - Are Tier 4 people becoming repeat collaborators?

7. VISUALIZE PATTERNS
   Create summary showing:
   - Top collaborators (by project count)
   - Project teams
   - Relationship clusters
   - Dependency risks
```

## Success Metrics

### Relationship Health Metrics

**Tier 1 (Core) Health:**
- Target: 100% contacted within 14 days
- Measure: `last_contact` date for all Tier 1
- Alert: If any Tier 1 > 14 days without contact

**Tier 2 (Strategic) Health:**
- Target: 90% contacted within 30 days
- Measure: `last_contact` date for all Tier 2
- Alert: If >2 Tier 2 people > 30 days without contact

**Tier Stability:**
- Measure: % of people remaining in same tier quarter-over-quarter
- Target: 70-80% stability (some movement is healthy)
- Alert: If >50% tier changes in single quarter (indicates assessment issues)

### Collaboration Metrics

**Strategic Partner Growth:**
- Measure: Number of Tier 2-3 people appearing in multiple projects
- Target: Increasing trend
- Success: Converting Tier 3 → Tier 2 based on multi-project collaboration

**Dependency Risk:**
- Measure: Number of critical projects with single-point dependencies
- Target: <20% of critical projects
- Alert: If >30% of projects depend on single person

**Relationship ROI:**
- Measure: Project success rate correlated with relationship tier
- Hypothesis: Projects with Tier 1-2 people should have higher success
- Track: Project health status by relationship tier of key contacts

## Best Practices

### Do's

✓ **Base tier assessment on data** - project count, interaction frequency, outcomes
✓ **Update regularly** - weekly reviews, after significant changes
✓ **Maintain proactively** - don't wait for Tier 1-2 people to reach out first
✓ **Document context** - person notes capture why relationships matter
✓ **Use Obsidian linking** - leverage graph view and backlinks
✓ **Track changes over time** - tier movements indicate relationship evolution
✓ **Balance investment** - More time on Tier 1-2, appropriate effort on Tier 3-4

### Don'ts

✗ **Don't over-tier people** - Most people should be Tier 3-4, reserve Tier 1-2 for truly strategic
✗ **Don't neglect data** - Feelings matter but data prevents bias
✗ **Don't create person notes for everyone** - Only Tier 1-3, maybe select Tier 4
✗ **Don't let Tier 1 relationships go stale** - These are critical, maintain actively
✗ **Don't ignore tier demotion signals** - Natural drift happens, adjust tiers accordingly
✗ **Don't make it personal** - Professional focus, objective assessment
✗ **Don't over-document** - Keep notes useful, not exhaustive

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** People list getting out of date

**Solution:**
- Run "Update people list" workflow weekly
- Set calendar reminder
- Integrate with weekly review process

---

**Problem:** Unsure what tier someone should be

**Solution:**
- Run "Assess relationship tier" workflow
- Review tier framework criteria
- Check project involvement + interaction data
- When in doubt, tier conservatively (lower tier)
- Can always promote later

---

**Problem:** Too many Tier 1 relationships

**Solution:**
- Tier 1 should be ~5-10 people maximum
- Review each against strict criteria
- Be honest about "critical to life/work" test
- Demote to Tier 2 if not truly foundational
- Quality over quantity for core relationships

---

**Problem:** Single-point dependency on critical project

**Solution:**
- Identify who else in organization could be backup
- Request introduction to secondary contact
- Attend meetings with both contacts
- Document knowledge in project notes
- Develop parallel relationship intentionally
